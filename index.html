Toolbox Talk: Site Supervisors

Loading trucks and getting your site ready

What has to be done before the truck turns up, and how doors, windows and metals come back to the yard.

Version 1.1 Updated 30 September 2026 Read time 4 min

Every minute a truck waits on site for material to be carried over and bagged is a minute it isn't on the next job. The load needs to be sitting ready in bulka bags, stillages or on pallets before the truck arrives. Crane on, crane off, gone.

This talk covers three things: getting your material ready for the booking, how aluminium doors and windows go in the stillages, and which metals come back to the yard and which never go on a Dapcor truck.

Topic 1

Have the load ready before the truck arrives

The booking is for lifting and leaving, not for collecting and packing. When the truck arrives the load should already be made up and sitting where the crane can reach it.

  • Make sure all rubbish is in a bulka bag, stillage or on a pallet before the truck arrives, not during the booking.
  • Nothing is carried in from another part of the site once the truck is on the ground. If it isn't bagged by then, it goes on the next booking.
  • Bags and pallets are made up where the crane can reach them, with clear access for the truck to set up.
  • Bags aren't overfilled and their lifting loops are intact and clear. Nothing sticking out the top that can catch on the way up.
  • The lift zone stays clear while the crane is working, and nobody stands under a load.

What the drivers have already been told

Drivers have had their own toolbox talk on this. They supervise the load, they don't do the manual loading, and they have been told plainly: Nothing loose is to be loaded. No loose material is to be delivered to the yard. A driver who stops loose material going on the truck is doing exactly what we have asked of them. Back them up.

Topic 2

Aluminium doors and windows

The stillages were bought for one job only: moving and storing doors and windows. They are not general rubbish cages. Units go in flat, stacked in layers, every one running the same way as the cross bars on the base. Doors run over 2000 mm long and 1250 mm wide against a stillage 1350 × 1070 internally, so they overhang the frame. That's normal and expected. What matters is that they all overhang the same way.

  • Stillages are for doors and windows. Don't fill them with general rubbish or offcuts.
  • Stack flat and all in one direction, the same direction as the cross bars on the base.
  • Overhang is expected, since the units are bigger than the frame. Keep it even at both ends and the same on every layer, so the stack sits square.
  • The top layer finishes at least 50 mm below the top of the stillage. They're stacked up to five high at the yard, each one's feet seated on the posts below. Load one proud and the stack stops there.
  • Nothing stood on edge, and no units crossed over each other at an angle.
  • The same rule applies to how they're stacked at the yard, so the stillage can be lifted by forklift at either end of the trip.

At the yard the units are disassembled and the material goes to the recyclers. Anything broken or packed wrong costs us that, and the glass ends up on the ground.

Topic 3

Metals: what comes back and what doesn't

Two different rules here, so don't mix them up. The valuable metals come back no matter how small the offcut. A 200 mm length of copper pipe goes back the same as a full coil. Steel is judged on quantity: a small amount can come back on a booking, a large amount gets a scrap merchant to site.

Back to the yard: any size, any weight

  • Copper piping
  • Brass
  • Stainless steel
  • Lead

Every offcut. Don't leave it in a bin or in a mixed bag. It's worth returning and it's worth stealing.

Steel: depends on the quantity

  • Roof sheeting
  • Colorbond
  • Steel fencing
  • Handrail

The any-size rule doesn't apply to these. A small amount can come back to the yard on a normal booking. A large amount is collected from site by a scrap merchant, not moved with Dapcor logistics.

  • Don't fill a booking with a large amount of steel. That's a job for a scrap merchant collecting from site. Arrange it rather than tying up a truck and a crane.
  • Don't mix the valuable metals in with general rubbish. Separate them on site, before the booking, while you can still see what's what.
  • Not sure whether yours counts as a small amount or a large one? Ring Ben before the booking rather than guessing at it on the day.
Topic 4

When the truck is on site

  • The driver supervises the load and operates the crane. The driver does not do the manual loading.
  • If a driver refuses loose material, that's the instruction they've been given. Don't argue it on site. Bag it and put it on the next booking.
  • Anything loose left on a truck has to come off before it leaves site.
  • Problems with a booking go to Ben Lawrence, Yard Manager, not to the driver.
